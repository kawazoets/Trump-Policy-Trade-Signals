# Case 04 — Nvidia: Trading Activity and U.S. AI-Chip Policy Toward China

## Research Question

This case examines whether Nvidia transactions in Donald Trump's disclosed investment accounts occurred in meaningful temporal proximity to U.S. policy changes affecting Nvidia's access to the Chinese AI-chip market.

Unlike earlier cases, Nvidia was both purchased and sold multiple times during the period.

The policy environment was also mixed rather than uniformly favorable to Nvidia.

Nvidia — Purchase

**$500,001–$1,000,000**

Reporting based on the financial disclosures indicates that Trump's accounts purchased at least $1.75 million of Nvidia shares during the quarter.

---

## China H200 Policy

Nvidia's H200 is an advanced AI accelerator whose sale to China is affected by U.S. export-control policy.

During early January 2026, the Trump Administration was moving toward allowing exports of H200 chips to China under a licensing system.

This potentially expanded Nvidia's access to a major market.

---

## Prior Public Information

The policy direction was not entirely secret at the time of the January 6 purchase.

By January 6, public reporting indicated that the U.S. government was actively working on licenses that would allow Nvidia H200 shipments to China.

Therefore:

**Trade Before Later Policy Action: YES**

but

**Trade Before Public Knowledge of Policy Direction: NOT ESTABLISHED**

This distinction is important.

---

## Additional Policy Action

The policy environment was not uniformly favorable to Nvidia.

On January 14, 2026, President Trump announced a Section 232 measure imposing a 25% tariff on certain advanced computing chips, including Nvidia's H200.

Certain U.S. uses, including domestic data-center applications, received exemptions or different treatment.

Therefore, Nvidia faced both:

- improved potential access to the Chinese market; and
- new trade-related costs and restrictions.

---

## Policy Direction

| Policy Development | Potential Nvidia Impact |
|---|---|
| H200 China export licensing | Positive |
| Greater access to Chinese AI market | Positive |
| 25% tariff on certain advanced chips | Negative / Mixed |
| Domestic data-center exemptions | Positive / Neutral |
| National-security review | Mixed |

The policy environment cannot accurately be classified as simply "pro-Nvidia."

Therefore, the Q1 transaction pattern was not a simple accumulation strategy.

---

## What Is Verified

- Nvidia shares were purchased multiple times during Q1 2026.
- A January 6 purchase was reported in the $500,001–$1,000,000 range.
- A February 10 purchase was reported in the $1,000,001–$5,000,000 range.
- Additional smaller Nvidia purchases occurred during February.
- Multiple Nvidia sales occurred during March.
- U.S. policy toward H200 exports to China changed during the same general period.
- Public information concerning potential H200 licensing existed by the time of the January 6 transaction.
- U.S. policy toward Nvidia included both potentially favorable and unfavorable elements.

---

## What Is Not Verified

The available public evidence does not establish:

- that investment managers knew nonpublic details of forthcoming Nvidia policy;
- that policy information was communicated from the Administration to the investment managers;
- that the Nvidia purchases were made because of forthcoming policy decisions;
- that the subsequent Nvidia sales were connected to government policy;
- the exact execution times of the disclosed transactions.

---

## Alternative Explanation

The Nvidia transactions occurred within a very large and actively managed portfolio.

Possible explanations include:

- systematic portfolio rebalancing;
- direct indexing;
- technology-sector allocation changes;
- publicly available information about AI-chip policy;
- ordinary profit taking;
- risk management after earlier purchases.

The existence of substantial Nvidia sales later in the quarter is particularly important counter-evidence against a simple theory of continuous policy-driven accumulation.

---

## Research Significance

Case 04 demonstrates another limitation of simple trade-versus-announcement analysis.

A company may be affected by several policy decisions moving in different economic directions, while the investment accounts may simultaneously make both purchases and sales.

Therefore, this project should not classify policy events simply as "favorable" or "unfavorable."

Future records should include a separate:

**Policy Direction**

field, using classifications such as:

- Positive
- Negative
- Mixed
- Neutral
- Uncertain

Case 04 also reinforces the importance of recording the full transaction history rather than selecting only purchases that precede favorable policy developments.
